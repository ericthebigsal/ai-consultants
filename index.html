Working definition · draft · September 2026

We get AI in software from playground to production

Most teams already have AI somewhere in the product or the process. Few have it at a quality they'd defend to a customer, an auditor, or their own on-call rotation. Closing that gap — between AI that demos and AI you can depend on — is the whole job. We're a new firm; the two founders aren't.

01

Why now

AI tooling crossed from novelty to genuinely production-capable in the last couple of years. Most organizations' adoption hasn't caught up with that — and the gap between what's possible and what's actually happening, disciplined, is the addressable market.

In the businesses we talk to, that gap shows up as one of a small number of patterns: no one's tried anything yet and doesn't know where to start; teams have tried plenty and none of it is trustworthy enough to depend on; spend is climbing with no one able to say whether it's buying anything; three different AI tools are in use with three different bills and no plan; or the SDLC itself hasn't been touched by any of it beyond autocomplete in an editor. Those five patterns are the first five pillars on Services. Three more — evaluation, trust and governance, and agentic reliability — run across all of them, and come up first for companies whose product itself is AI. We built the eight around what we keep seeing, not to make a tidy list.

02

Eight ways we help

Every one of them serves the same end — AI you can actually depend on. The first five form a journey — engaging one tends to surface the next. The last three run across the whole loop. Each is its own engagement with its own shape; full detail, including what we explicitly don't do, is on Services.

Pillar 01 AI orientation & opportunity mapping You've heard AI could help. We find the specific, valuable places it actually would — in your delivery process or the product itself — ranked, not hand-waved. Pillar 02 From playground to production Your teams are already experimenting. We turn that into tools you'd trust in front of a customer. Pillar 03 AI cost efficiency Spend is climbing faster than anyone can explain. We find out why, and put a ceiling on it. Pillar 04 Platform consolidation Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, and no plan. We do the hands-on work of standardizing on one, without losing what works. Pillar 05 AI in the SDLC We integrate AI across define, build, test, release, and maintain — the whole cycle, not just the editor. Pillar 06 · across the loop Evaluation & quality systems "How do we know it's any good?" answered with a number. Eval sets from real usage, a harness in CI and on live traffic, a bar you can show a customer. Pillar 07 · across the loop AI trust & governance The register, the enforced control set, the framework crosswalk — and the answers that get an enterprise AI questionnaire cleared without a fire drill. Pillar 08 · across the loop Agentic systems & reliability Works in the demo, 85% in production. We design the agent to fail safe — abstain, escalate, trace — and measure the reliability, not assert it.
03

How we work, in brief

We don't sell a pillar before we understand the business. Every engagement starts with a discovery conversation against a fixed set of questions (see Problem space & discovery), followed by a short assessment and a written roadmap tied to a measurable outcome. From there it's either advisory work, hands-on build work, or both, depending on what the roadmap actually calls for — and it ends with enablement, not a dependency on us.
