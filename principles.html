Our point of view

What "judicious" AI use means to us

A starting set, drafted from how we've talked about this so far. Proposed, not ratified — if you'd cut one, add one, or word one differently, that's the point of this page.

Production quality, not playground quality. Proposed

If an AI-assisted feature reaches a customer, it earns the same review, testing, and on-call ownership as anything else you ship. "It's just AI" is not an exception — it doesn't get more slack, and it doesn't get more hype.

Judicious, not maximal. Proposed

The goal isn't the most AI. It's AI in the highest-leverage places and nowhere else. We'll tell a client when the honest answer is "don't build this."

Measure before, measure after. Proposed

Every engagement starts with a baseline and ends with a before/after number. If we can't measure it, we don't know whether it worked, and neither does the client.

Don't ship what you can't maintain. Proposed

A clever AI feature nobody on the team can debug at 2am is a liability wearing a demo's clothing. We optimize for what the client's own team can own after we leave.

The model is not the moat. Proposed

Vendor and model choice matter far less than most companies think. We help build workflows and evaluation practices that survive a model swap, not ones locked to one vendor's quirks.

People and process before tools. Proposed

Tool sprawl and playground code are usually symptoms of missing direction, not missing technology. We fix the direction first.

We say what we'd do if it were our company. Proposed

No recommendation we wouldn't act on ourselves. If the honest advice costs us the engagement, we say it anyway.

Enablement over dependency. Proposed

Success looks like the client not needing us for the next one. We document, train, and hand off by default — see Engagement model.
