Analysis 03

Does the AI version actually win?

For every feature, a held-out test set and a deterministic baseline — a rule, a heuristic, a search. The AI version only stays if it beats the baseline by enough to justify the cost. Two don't.

Win rate = share of a 500-item blind test where reviewers (or a downstream metric) preferred the AI output over the baseline.

The two cuts

Segment suggester — 43% win rate

The AI suggestions were preferred less than half the time against a rules-based recommender built from purchase recency, category, and engagement. It also cost $0.02 a call and made 340k calls a month. Cutting it removes a feature that was, on balance, making segmentation slightly worse.

Monthly cost$6.8k→$0The rules-based recommender it lost to is kept and promoted to the default.

Send-time predictor — 38% win rate

A per-recipient "send at the hour they usually open" heuristic beat the model 62% of the time and costs nothing to run. The model was added because it sounded better, not because it tested better — and no one had tested it.

Monthly cost$8.0k→$0The heuristic becomes the shipping behaviour.

What good looks like

  • Every AI feature has a named baseline and a test set checked into the repo — if you can't say what it's better than, it doesn't ship.
  • The eval suite runs in CI on every prompt or model change, and on a schedule to catch drift.
  • A feature that drops below its threshold opens a ticket, not a silent regression.
  • “It feels better” is not a verdict. The verdict is a number against a baseline.
This is the gate for the routing plan. No feature on the routing table is downgraded until its eval confirms the cheaper tier holds the win rate.
