Brand guidelines · v1.0

Northwind sounds like a colleague who has read the data.

Precise, plain, and unbothered. We help you get to the numbers and understand them; we don't perform excitement about them. This guide is short on purpose — a brand people won't read is a brand people won't follow.

4 voice principles 30 worked string rewrites 1 wordmark, 2 lockups Checked before every release

01 · Identity

The wordmark

One wordmark. The compass mark points north; the split color carries the “wind.” No separate icon-only logo — at small sizes the mark alone reads as a generic arrow.

Northwind
Enhance Rendered the wordmark against 40 backgrounds and at 12 sizes to find where contrast and the mark break down; produced the clear-space and minimum-size rules from the failures. the minimum-size call, and the one dark-background lockup we allow.

Rules

  • Clear space: the height of the compass mark on all sides.
  • Minimum size: 96px wide on screen, 22mm in print. Below that, use the mark alone.
  • Color: ink + petrol on light grounds; all-surface (#FBFAF6) on petrol or ink grounds. No other combinations.
Do
Place it on color.ground or color.surface with full clear space.
Avoid
Recolor the mark, stretch the wordmark, or set it on a photograph.

02 · Color

Brand color

The palette is the design system's — brand doesn't get its own colors. What brand owns is meaning: petrol is Northwind, signal-red is only ever a warning.

Petrol
#1B4B49
The brand color. Primary actions, the wordmark, one accent per screen.
Chalk
#F2F1EC
The ground. Calm, matte, never pure white.
Signal
#D6452B
Warnings only. If it isn't a problem, it isn't this color.
Restraint is the brand. One petrol accent per view. A screen that's mostly chalk and ink, with a single confident mark of color, is on brand. A screen with four accent colors is not.

03 · Voice

How Northwind writes

Four principles. Each has worked examples, because a principle without one is just an adjective.

Create Read ~400 support replies, release notes, and marketing pages; pulled out the recurring phrasings and which ones customers responded well to; drafted principles from the pattern. the four that survived, and cutting the two that amounted to “be friendly.”

1 · Lead with the number

Do
“Weekly active accounts rose 4.2%, against a 3.0% plan.”
Avoid
“We're thrilled to share some exciting growth this week!”

2 · Say what changed, then what to do

Do
“Trial-to-paid fell 0.8 points. The drop is all in self-serve signups — check the onboarding email that changed Tuesday.”
Avoid
“Conversion metrics are trending in a concerning direction and warrant further investigation.”

3 · Errors explain and instruct

Do
“This report needs a date range. Pick a start and end date, then run it again.”
Avoid
“Oops! Something went wrong. Please try again later.”

4 · No performed enthusiasm

Do
“Scheduled reports are here. Set one up from any saved view.”
Avoid
“🎉 We can't wait for you to try our game-changing new Scheduled Reports!”
Accelerate Rewrote 30 real in-product strings — empty states, errors, toasts — against each principle, so every rule ships with a before/after from the actual product. which rewrites went live; a few were fine as they were.

04 · Governance

Keeping it current

A brand guide is only real if it's enforced somewhere. Northwind's runs as a check before copy ships, not a PDF someone forgot about.

Maintain Reads new marketing pages, release notes, and lifecycle emails on the way to publish; flags any copy that breaks a voice principle, with the principle and a suggested fix. tone judgment on anything borderline — the check flags, a person decides.
  • Pre-publish check. Customer-facing copy is checked against the four principles before it ships. Flags are advisory, not blocking.
  • Quarterly read. The principles are re-checked against a fresh sample of what customers actually responded to. If one stops matching reality, it changes.
  • Ownership. One person owns the voice. New rules are proposed in writing with examples.
What we hand over. This guide, the 30 rewrites as a reference set, the pre-publish check configured for your CMS, and a working session so your writers own it.
