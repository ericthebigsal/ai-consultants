Why these four columns

What actually predicts trouble

Not model choice or prompt length — those are downstream. Four things predict whether an AI feature is safe to depend on, and every one of the six is missing at least two.

  • Where the output goes. A draft an agent edits is low-stakes. Something auto-sent to a customer, or that changes state they see, is not. The bar scales with this.
  • Does it have an eval. Without one, “is it good?” is answered by whoever looked last and how they felt about it. Five of six have nothing; the sixth has an ad-hoc notebook that doesn't run in CI.
  • Is a human in the loop. Not as a permanent crutch — as the thing you can remove later, deliberately, once the eval earns it.
  • Who owns it. Two features have no name attached. An AI feature with no owner has no one to catch the day its help-center source changes and its answers quietly go stale.
What this unlocks. The inventory is what makes the next step possible: a production bar written against real gaps, not a generic checklist, and a verdict on each feature that the owning team can actually defend.
