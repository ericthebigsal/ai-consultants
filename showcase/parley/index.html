Pillar 02 · From playground to production

Six AI features shipped. Two are good, one is a liability, and nobody could say which was which.

Three teams at Parley added AI to the product over eighteen months, each to its own standard or none. This engagement puts a production bar in writing, holds every feature to it, and sorts the six into keep, harden, and retire — then hardens one all the way, to show what the verdict actually costs.

6 features · 3 teams 0 evaluated in CI 2 keep · 2 harden · 2 retire same review bar as any code

The situation, in the client's words

“Half our engineers are already using AI, and half our roadmap now has AI in it. Some of it’s great. Some of it I’m pretty sure is a liability. The problem is I can’t tell you which is which, and neither can the people who built it.”

— VP Engineering, Parley

What the engagement produces

Four deliverables, and one hardening worked end to end

Deliverable 01 The inventory Every AI surface, where its output lands, whether it has an eval, whether a human is in the loop, when it was last reviewed. Deliverable 02 The production bar Five rules for shipping AI-assisted work, folded into Parley's existing PR review, each with a pass and a fail from the six. Deliverable 03 Keep / harden / retire Every feature sorted against the bar, with the reason and what acting on the verdict costs. Deliverable 04 The enablement kit The CI eval gate, the review checklist in the PR template, a pattern library from Parley's own code, the quarterly re-check. Worked example The answer bot, demo to dependency The riskiest feature taken all the way through hardening — failure-mode catalogue, a retrofitted eval, guardrails, before / after.

How to read this

What's illustrative

Parley is a fictional company. The six features, the failure rates, the verdicts, the eval numbers, and the before / after figures are invented to show the shape of a playground-to-production engagement — what an audit surfaces, what a production bar contains, how features get sorted against it, and what hardening one actually involves. Where a page shows model output, it is pre-generated for fixed inputs and marked as such. Nothing here calls a model.

The line this engagement holds. Nothing here is anti-AI. The point is that an AI-assisted feature earns the same trust as any other code the same way — a written bar, an eval, a review, an owner — not a special exception and not a special fear.

This is an illustrative sample, not a real engagement. Parley is a fictional company; the AI features, the failure rates, the verdicts, and every figure are invented to show the shape of the work.
