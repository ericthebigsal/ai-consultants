Orientation — the starting point

Where would AI actually help this platform?

Before anyone built anything, this was the question. We interviewed across product, engineering, support, and customer success, looked at how wineries actually use the platform, and scored every candidate against effort, risk, and customer value. Four made the cut. This is the map, and the reasoning — including what didn’t make it.

12 candidates considered 4 recommended · 1 sequenced later · 7 out ~1.5-week engagement

01 · How we looked

The method

A short diagnostic, not a build. The point is a ranked list the leadership team can defend without us in the room — not a technology survey.

  • Interviews. Product, engineering, support, and customer success — where does the platform already lose customers time to repetitive, well-understood work?
  • Usage data. Which features are underused because the interface is hard, which support tickets repeat, where wineries drop off.
  • Scoring. Each candidate against three axes — not against what’s technically impressive.

The three axes

EffortRough build weeks, including the platform work the capability forces (API, data model, evals).
RiskWhat can go wrong, and how bad — a wrong answer to a customer, a quality regression, a data-handling issue.
Customer valueEffect on winery retention, revenue per winery, and whether it’s a reason someone chooses Chateau Salerno over a competitor.

03 · What made the cut

The four, ranked — with the case for each

Each one: what it costs to try, what it’s worth if it works, and how you’d know within weeks — not quarters.

#1 — Natural-language segmentation

Cost to try: ~3 weeks, on the segment engine that already exists. Worth if it works: fewer “how do I build this segment” support tickets, more wineries reaching the full power of the engine, and a live differentiator in sales demos. How you’d know in 6 weeks: ship it behind a flag to 5 pilot wineries — segments created per week up, segmentation support tickets down, and 3 of the 5 say they’d miss it.

See the prototype

#2 — Churn & LTV prediction

Cost to try: ~5 weeks — a scoring pipeline on club history plus a factor-attribution layer. Worth if it works: wineries retain club members they would have lost, their success on the platform improves, and Chateau Salerno’s own churn drops. The clearest measurable return of the four. How you’d know: run it silently for a quarter, compare predicted vs. actual cancels; then a pilot win-back journey with a holdout group.

See the prototype

#3 — Campaign & journey drafting

Cost to try: ~4 weeks — a journey-definition API and a voice model built from the winery’s past sends. Worth if it works: one-person winery marketing teams ship the multi-step journeys only larger wineries manage today, driving more DTC revenue and making the platform stickier. How you’d know: journeys published per winery up; the drafted-then-actually-shipped rate.

See the prototype

#4 — Tasting-note & product copy

Cost to try: ~2 weeks — bind generation to the structured product fields already in the platform. Worth if it works: a recurring chore removed every release, higher day-to-day satisfaction, better renewals and referrals. Lowest stakes of the four — a good place to build confidence. How you’d know: copy tasks completed per release, time per release down, a short satisfaction check with pilot wineries.

See the prototype

The test of a good map: the Chateau Salerno leadership team can name these four, put them in this order, and defend the ranking to their board without us in the room.

04 · What didn't, and why

The cut list

Often the most useful part. Every one of these is a real idea; none of them is the right first move for this platform.

CandidateCallWhy
Predictive allocation planningLaterGenuinely valuable — “you’ll sell out of the Rosé by May, hold 40 cases for the club.” But it needs the club-history data foundation that #2 builds. Sequenced after, not cut.
Storefront product recommendationsOutA rules-based version — recency, varietal, club tier — captures most of the value at a fraction of the effort and risk. Not an AI differentiator.
Support chatbot for winery operatorsOutSupport volume per winery is low; a better help center and in-product guidance win first. Revisit if support load grows.
Post-purchase feedback / NPS analysisOutData volumes per winery are small and the insight per winery is thin. Not a reason anyone buys the platform.
Send-time optimizationOutA per-recipient “send at the hour they usually open” heuristic captures most of the value and costs nothing to run. A model here is effort spent for a rounding error.
Fraud / bad-actor detectionOutA trust-and-operations project, not a marketing differentiator — and the cost of a false positive, blocking a real customer, is high. A separate initiative with its own owner.
Media-library auto-taggingOutReal but tiny. Worth doing eventually as a quality-of-life feature; never worth a program.
AI questionnaire builderOutAdjacent to the core — the feedback module — with a small audience. The same pattern as #1, applied to a less valuable surface.

05 · The recommended first move

Start with natural-language segmentation

Not because it’s the highest-value — #2 has the clearer ROI — but because it’s the fastest to prove, the easiest to show a prospect, and it establishes the pattern every later capability reuses.

  • Lowest effort of the four — it runs on the segment engine that already exists.
  • Immediately demoable — “describe your audience” is a live moment in a sales call.
  • De-risks the rest — it proves the core pattern: the model writes a normal, editable platform object, and a person reviews it. Every other capability on the map works the same way.

The 6-week proof

Build it against the segment engine, ship behind a flag to 5 pilot wineries, and measure: segments created per week, segmentation support tickets, and whether 3 of the 5 wineries say they’d miss it if it went away. If two of those three signals move, the rest of the map is worth building. If they don’t, we’ve spent six weeks, not a program.
